## Problem

Some synthetic chemicals do not break down: PFAS, microplastics, some pesticides and flame retardants. They are now present in soil, rainwater, wildlife and human blood everywhere on earth.

Because they persist, the stock keeps growing even if emissions stop. The decision to release them is effectively permanent.

Harm is diffuse, delayed and hard to trace to one emitter, so no one is liable and nothing stops. The cost is paid by people who never chose it.

Several of these substances disrupt hormones and are suspected in falling sperm counts and fertility. See [health](../health/) and [demographics](../demographics/).

Air pollution from combustion already kills millions per year. See [energy](../energy/).

## Research

### Why PFAS do not break down

The carbon-fluorine bond has a dissociation energy of about 485 kJ/mol, the strongest single bond in organic chemistry. A fully fluorinated chain offers no site for an enzyme or a hydroxyl radical to attack. No meaningful environmental degradation pathway is known for perfluoroalkyl acids like PFOA and PFOS.

How many PFAS exist depends on the definition. The OECD's 2018 market inventory listed [4,730 CAS numbers](https://one.oecd.org/document/ENV/JM/MONO%282018%297/en/pdf); EPA's structure-based list held [over 21,000 substances as of January 2026](https://comptox.epa.gov/dashboard/chemical-lists/PFASSTRUCT). Almost none have toxicity data.

They also leave the body slowly. [Li et al. (2018)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29133598/) measured mean serum half-lives in 106 residents of Ronneby, Sweden, after firefighting-foam contamination of their water ended: 2.7 years (PFOA), 3.4 years (PFOS) and 5.3 years (PFHxS).

### Limits, falling and rising

* EPA 2016 lifetime health advisory: 70 ppt, PFOA + PFOS combined.
* EPA [June 2022 interim advisories](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/06/21/2022-13158/lifetime-drinking-water-health-advisories-for-four-perfluoroalkyl-substances): 0.004 ppt PFOA (17,500x lower) and 0.02 ppt PFOS (3,500x lower), below routine detection.
* EPA [April 2024 enforceable limits](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas): 4 ppt each for PFOA and PFOS, 10 ppt for PFHxS, PFNA and HFPO-DA (GenX).
* EU [Drinking Water Directive 2020/2184](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2020/2184/oj/eng): 0.10 µg/L (100 ppt) for the sum of 20 PFAS, from 12 January 2026 — far looser per compound.
* But in May 2026 EPA [proposed rescinding](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/20/2026-10085/rescission-of-regulatory-determinations-and-removal-of-related-provisions-for-four-pfas-substances) the PFHxS, PFNA, HFPO-DA and hazard-index limits, and delaying PFOA/PFOS compliance toward 2031.

[Cousins et al. (2022)](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.2c02765) found PFOA in rainwater routinely above the EPA advisory level worldwide, including Antarctica and the Tibetan Plateau. Their planetary-boundary claim depends on the guideline chosen: the 2022 advisories are low enough that nearly any water fails.

Levels respond to policy: US [NHANES serum data](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10648322/) show PFOS down over 85% and PFOA over 70% from 1999 to 2018 after producers phased them out; replacements, themselves PFAS, rose.

### Microplastics

[Eriksen et al. (2023)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0281596) estimate 82-358 trillion particles (mean 171 trillion) weighing 1.1-4.9 million tonnes (mean 2.3) afloat at the ocean surface in 2019. The four-fold spread is the finding: trawl nets cut off near 300 µm, wind mixes plastic below the sampled layer, and coverage is sparse. [WHO's 2019 review](https://sapea.info/sapea-evidence-review-helps-shape-new-who-microplastics-report/) found no demonstrated risk at measured drinking-water levels, and could not rule one out.

### Endocrine disruption and sperm counts

[Vandenberg et al. (2012)](https://academic.oup.com/edrv/article-abstract/33/3/378/2354852) reviewed over 800 studies arguing that hormone-active chemicals often show non-monotonic dose-response: an effect at low dose, none at mid dose, a different effect high up. If so, testing high doses and extrapolating downward — the basis of most safety assessment — misses real harm, and no safe threshold is definable. Regulators and industry contest the reproducibility; unresolved as of 2026, and the main reason this class resists regulation.

[Levine et al. (2017)](https://academic.oup.com/humupd/article/23/6/646/4035689) reported a 52.4% fall in sperm concentration among unselected Western men, 1973-2011. The [2022 update](https://academic.oup.com/humupd/article/29/2/157/6824414), 53 countries and 57,168 men, 1973-2018, found 51.6% overall, -1.16% per year, accelerating to -2.64% after 2000. [Boulicault et al. (2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33969777/) counter that the pooled data mix clinic and population samples across decades of changing counting methods, are thin outside Europe and North America, and assume higher counts mean better health. The decline is contested; attribution to any named chemical is unestablished.

### Air pollution

From GBD 2021, via [State of Global Air 2024](https://www.stateofglobalair.org/news-events/2024/new-state-global-air-report-finds-air-pollution-second-leading-risk-factor-death):

* 8.1 million deaths attributable to air pollution in 2021, the second-leading risk factor for death worldwide.
* Over 90% of that burden from PM2.5, ambient and household combined; ozone about 489,000.
* 709,000 deaths in children under five, roughly 500,000 from household burning of solid fuels.
* About 99% of people live above WHO's 2021 guideline of 5 µg/m³ annual mean PM2.5.

These are attributable deaths, not death certificates: modelled exposure run through exposure-response functions, where the choice of function and counterfactual moves the total by millions. See [energy](../energy/) and [health](../health/).
