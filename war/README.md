## Problem

About 12000 nuclear warheads exist. A large exchange would cause crop failure worldwide, killing far more people outside the belligerent countries than inside them. See [food](../food/).

Arms control treaties are lapsing, more states hold weapons, warning times shrink and decisions fall to fewer people under more automation. See [ai](../ai/).

Autonomous weapons are cheap, easy to produce in numbers and hard to attribute, which lowers the threshold for using them.

Scarcity of [water](../water/), food and [resources](../resources/), plus mass [migration](../migration/), adds pressure between states that already hold nuclear weapons.

Deterrence has held for 80 years, but it only has to fail once. See [coordination](../coordination/).

## Research

### Who holds what

[SIPRI](https://www.sipri.org/yearbook/2026/08) and the [FAS Nuclear Notebook](https://fas.org/initiative/status-world-nuclear-forces/) count about 12,187 warheads in nine states in January 2026. Roughly 9,745 are in military stockpiles, the rest retired and queued for dismantlement. About 4,012 are deployed on missiles or at bomber bases, of which 2,100-2,200 sit on high alert. Stockpiles by state:

* Russia 4,400 (5,420 total inventory), United States 3,700 (5,042)
* China 620, France 290, United Kingdom 225
* India 190, Pakistan 170, Israel 90, North Korea 60

Russia and the United States hold about 86% of all warheads. The total is falling only because those two dismantle retired stock faster than new warheads enter service, which SIPRI expects to reverse. China is growing fastest.

### Treaties

New START [expired on 5 February 2026](https://www.armscontrol.org/act/2026-03/news/new-start-expires-us-urges-modernized-treaty). For the first time since 1972 there is no binding ceiling on US and Russian strategic forces and no mutual inspection. Russia offered in September 2025 to observe the old limits informally for a year; no agreement followed.

The 2026 NPT Review Conference ended with no final document, the third in a row. The [CTBT](https://www.armscontrol.org/factsheets/status-comprehensive-test-ban-treaty-signatories-and-ratifiers) is still not in force, eight Annex 2 states short, and Russia revoked its ratification in 2023. The US has not tested explosively since 1992. The [TPNW](https://www.icanw.org/signature_and_ratification_status) had about 75 states parties in mid-2026, no weapon state among them.

### What a large exchange would do

Modelling by Robock, Toon, Coupe and Xia puts 100 Hiroshima-sized airbursts on Indian and Pakistani cities at about 5 Tg of stratospheric soot, a US-Russia exchange at about 150 Tg. Sunlight lofts the soot higher still and it stays for years. 5 Tg gives over 1 K of global cooling, roughly 10% less precipitation and heavy ozone loss; 150 Tg gives below-freezing Northern Hemisphere summers ([review](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/23/6691/2023/)).

[Xia et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-022-00573-0) fed six soot scenarios into crop, fishery and livestock models. Anything above 5 Tg causes mass food shortages in almost every country: over 2 billion starvation deaths in the India-Pakistan case, over 5 billion at 150 Tg. See [food](../food/).

The soot figure is contested. [Reisner et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017JD027331) at Los Alamos simulated the fires in detail and got only 0.2-1.5 Tg of black carbon above 12 km from 100 detonations. Robock's group replied that the model omits latent heat and under-lofts the plume. The dispute is over how much soot reaches the stratosphere, not what it does there.

### Close calls

Documented at the [National Security Archive](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/nuclear-vault/2020-03-16/false-warnings-soviet-missile-attacks-during-1979-80-led-alert-actions-us-strategic-forces):

* 9 November 1979: a training tape loaded into the live NORAD system showed 1,400 incoming ICBMs; interceptors launched, the error was caught in about six minutes.
* Mid-1980: a faulty 46-cent chip reported 2,020 ICBMs; SAC bomber crews boarded with engines running.
* 26 September 1983: Stanislav Petrov did not pass on a Soviet satellite warning of five US launches.
* 25 January 1995: a Norwegian scientific rocket ran the Russian alert chain up to the president's command briefcase.

### Autonomous weapons

The UN CCW [Group of Governmental Experts](https://meetings.unoda.org/ccw-/convention-on-certain-conventional-weapons-group-of-governmental-experts-on-lethal-autonomous-weapons-systems-2026) has discussed lethal autonomous weapons since 2014 and works by consensus, so any one state can block an outcome; Russia and the United States both oppose a binding instrument. Its 2019 output was eleven non-binding guiding principles. 2026 is the mandate's final year, and the rolling draft text is still defining terms. See [ai](../ai/).

### Conflict trends

[UCDP](https://ucdp.uu.se/) recorded about 244,600 deaths in organised violence in 2025, up from roughly 187,000 in 2024 and the second-highest since 1994. State-based fighting was about 153,600 of that, with Russia-Ukraine alone at some 62% of battle deaths. UCDP counted 65 state-based conflicts, the most since records began in 1946, 8 of them between states, the most since the Second World War, and 13 above the 1,000-death threshold for war, the most since 1992. The long post-1990s decline in war deaths ended around 2020.
