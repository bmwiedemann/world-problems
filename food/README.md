## Problem

Yield growth of the major cereals is slowing while demand keeps rising.

High yields rest on two mined or synthesised inputs. Nitrogen fertiliser is made from natural gas, so its price follows [energy](../energy/). Phosphate is mined from a few deposits and has no substitute at all. See [resources](../resources/).

Topsoil erodes faster than it forms. Salinisation and falling water tables take irrigated land out of production. See [water](../water/).

The world eats a handful of genetically narrow crops. Heat above flowering thresholds cuts yields sharply, and simultaneous harvest failure in several breadbaskets is plausible within this century. See [climate change](../climate-change/) and [biodiversity](../biodiversity/).

Most countries import calories from a small number of exporters. When harvests fail, exporters ban exports first, which turns a regional shortfall into a global one. See [politics](../politics/).

Stocks cover weeks, not years.

## Research

### Yield growth

Ray et al. fitted trends to global harvest statistics for 1961-2008: annual yield gains of 1.6% for maize, 1.3% for soybean, 1.0% for rice and 0.9% for wheat. Doubling production by 2050 would need about 2.4% per year, and on roughly a quarter to a third of harvested area yields had stagnated or fallen outright ([PLOS ONE, 2013](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0066428)).

Grassini et al. showed the trends are linear rather than exponential: the absolute gain per hectare holds steady, so the percentage gain shrinks every year. They also found outright plateaus in rice in eastern Asia and wheat in northwest Europe, regions accounting for about 31% of global rice, wheat and maize production ([Nature Communications, 2013](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms3918)). Those regions sit closest to the agronomic ceiling; further growth must come from closing yield gaps elsewhere.

### The two mined inputs

* Erisman et al. estimated that synthetic nitrogen from the Haber-Bosch process fed 48% of the world population as of 2008 ([Nature Geoscience, 2008](https://www.nature.com/articles/ngeo325)). That share is a mass-balance estimate, not a measurement.
* Ammonia synthesis consumed about 170 billion m³ of natural gas in 2020, roughly 20% of industrial gas demand and about 2% of global final energy; over 70% of output comes from gas-based steam reforming, most of the rest from coal ([IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap, 2021](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary)). Fertiliser price tracks gas price. See [energy](../energy/).
* Phosphate rock: world mine production about 240 Mt in 2024; reserves about 74 billion tonnes, of which 50 billion — two thirds — sit in Morocco and Western Sahara ([USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2025/mcs2025-phosphate.pdf)). "Reserves" is an economic category, revised sharply upward in past editions, which is why published peak-phosphorus dates span more than a century. See [resources](../resources/).

### Soil

Montgomery's compilation of measured rates found conventionally ploughed fields losing soil one to two orders of magnitude faster than soil forms, while no-till fields erode close to the rate of soil production ([PNAS, 2007](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0611508104)). Global modelled erosion was about 35.9 Pg/yr in 2012 — an estimate its authors note is at least half the older reference figures, which shows how weak the data is ([Nature Communications, 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-02142-7)).

### Heat

Pooling field-warming experiments, process models and statistical regressions, Zhao et al. put yield loss per +1 °C of global mean temperature at 7.4% for maize, 6.0% for wheat, 3.2% for rice and 3.1% for soybean, before CO2 fertilisation, adaptation or breeding ([PNAS, 2017](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1701762114)). How much CO2 fertilisation offsets is the live disagreement.

Failures correlate. Amplified Rossby waves (wavenumbers 5 and 7) raise the probability of simultaneous heat extremes across widely separated breadbaskets by up to 20-fold for the most severe events ([Nature Climate Change, 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-019-0637-z)), and the risk of simultaneous failure rises disproportionately between 1.5 °C and 2 °C, most of all for wheat ([Nature Climate Change, 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-019-0600-z)). Models likely understate this ([Nature Communications, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-38906-7)).

### Markets

Martin and Anderson attributed about 45% of the 2007-08 rise in world rice prices, and about 30% of the wheat rise, to export restrictions rather than the underlying shortfall ([World Bank WPS 5645, 2011](https://www.imf.org/external/np/seminars/eng/2011/trade/pdf/session1-martin-paper.pdf)). The FAO Food Price Index hit a nominal record of 159.7 points in March 2022, about 17% above the 2011 peak, though in real terms below 1974 ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/worldfoodsituation/foodpricesindex/en/)).

Stocks are not the buffer they look like. FAO put the world cereal stocks-to-use ratio at 32.0% for 2026/27 as of July 2026, on 2,983 Mt of production against 2,961 Mt of utilisation ([FAO Cereal Supply and Demand Brief](https://www.fao.org/worldfoodsituation/csdb/en)). A large share sits in China, is not offered to the world market, and is itself only estimated; the tradeable ratio is well below the headline.
