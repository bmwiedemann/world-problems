## Problem

Global energy consumption has seen an exponential growth.
If this were to continue, it would soon surpass the amount of available energy (in some decades?).

Fossil fuels are problematic, because the CO2 emissions cause [climate change](../climate-change/)

Waste heat sets a hard ceiling: any energy used on earth ends up as heat, independent of the source.

Building non-fossil energy needs large amounts of metal, and grid-scale storage more so. See [resources](../resources/).

Thermal plants need cooling water, hydro needs rivers, and both are affected by drought. See [water](../water/).

## Research

### How big, and how fast

* Global total energy supply passed 600 EJ (about 167,000 TWh) in 2025, up 1.7% on 2024 — [Energy Institute, Statistical Review of World Energy](https://www.energyinst.org/statistical-review).
* Averaged over 1965 to 2025 the growth rate was about 2.3% per year. Over 2015 to 2025 it was about 1.4% per year (computed from [Energy Institute data via Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/primary-energy-consumption-by-source)).

So the growth is exponential but slow: a doubling every 30 to 50 years, not every decade.

* Fossil share of supply: 97.4% in 1965, 89.6% in 2005, 86.2% in 2025 ([data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/fossil-fuels-share-energy)).
* Between 2015 and 2025 total supply grew by about 21,000 TWh. Fossil fuels supplied about 14,100 TWh of that growth, solar and wind about 4,400 TWh.

Solar and wind have grown fast in percentage terms and have still mostly been added on top of fossil fuels rather than displacing them. Data centres are a new increment: about 485 TWh in 2025, growing 17% that year ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/news/data-centre-electricity-use-surged-in-2025-even-with-tightening-bottlenecks-driving-a-scramble-for-solutions)). See [ai](../ai/).

### Why the accounting changes the answer

A coal plant burns roughly 3 units of heat per unit of electricity. Counting the heat for coal but only the electricity for solar makes renewables look smaller than they are; the [substitution method](https://ourworldindata.org/energy-substitution-method) corrects for this, though agencies differ in applying it. The energy that actually does work is smaller still: [Aramendia et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-024-01518-6) put fossil fuels' useful-stage energy return on investment near 3.5:1, against about 8.5:1 at the final-fuel stage.

### Costs

* Solar modules have followed a learning rate near 20% per doubling of cumulative production for four decades ([Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/learning-curve)). Module prices reached roughly $0.07 to $0.09 per watt in 2024 to early 2025, then rose as Chinese producers cut output. A learning curve is a fitted trend, not a law.
* Battery pack prices averaged $108/kWh in 2025, down about 8% on 2024; stationary storage packs came in near $70/kWh ([BloombergNEF](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-transport/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour-despite-rising-metal-prices-bloombergnef/)).

### What storage would have to do

About 108 GW of batteries were added worldwide in 2025, roughly 40% more than 2024 ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-energy-review-2026/technology-battery-storage)), for a cumulative stock near 270 GW / 630 GWh ([Wood Mackenzie](https://www.woodmac.com/news/opinion/global-energy-storage-market-surpasses-100-gw-annual-installation-milestone-in-2025/)). World electricity use is roughly 32,000 TWh a year, about 88 TWh a day. The entire global battery fleet therefore holds under ten minutes of world electricity demand, and most projects are built for two to four hours. Batteries handle the day-night cycle. The multi-day windless spell and the winter deficit are a different problem.

### The heat ceiling

[Flanner 2009](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2008GL036465) put the global radiative forcing from human waste heat at +0.028 W/m², against roughly 2.9 W/m² from greenhouse gases — about 1%, but +0.39 over the United States and +0.68 over western Europe. Waste heat scales with energy use, not with emissions, so it does not go away with decarbonisation. At 2.3% per year, use rises tenfold per century; [Tom Murphy's arithmetic](https://dothemath.ucsd.edu/2011/07/galactic-scale-energy/) puts the boiling point around 400 years out. It is a floor no energy source removes.

### Where it is contested

* Net energy. [Brockway et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-019-0425-z) found fossil EROI at the finished-fuel stage near 6:1 and falling about 23% over 16 years, implying a net energy cliff. Aramendia et al. 2024 put the break-even for renewables near 4.6:1 and conclude wind and solar already clear it.
* Efficiency. [Brockway et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rser.2021.110781) find economy-wide rebound may erode more than half of the expected savings from efficiency gains. Efficiency reduces cost, cost raises use. See [economics](../economics/).
* Weak data. Traditional biomass, roughly 11,000 TWh, is estimated rather than measured. Chinese coal figures are revised often, by large amounts.
