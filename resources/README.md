## Problem

We mine the easy deposits first. Ore grades fall over time, so each ton of metal needs more rock moved, more [energy](../energy/) and more [water](../water/).

Electrifying everything needs copper, lithium, nickel and rare earths far faster than mines can open. From discovery to production takes 10 to 20 years, so a shortage cannot be answered quickly.

Mining and especially refining are concentrated in few countries, which makes supply a political lever rather than a market. See [politics](../politics/).

Some materials are dissipated rather than consumed and cannot be recovered: phosphorus spread on fields and washed into the sea, helium lost to space.

Recycling can supply a stable stock but not a growing one.

Cheap materials are an assumption in nearly every proposed solution elsewhere in this project. See [economics](../economics/).

## Research

**Reserves are an inventory, not the size of the deposit.** The USGS defines reserves as the part of identified resources extractable economically at the time of the estimate, and says "reserves data are dynamic": they usually grow as deposits are explored, technology improves or prices rise ([USGS MCS 2026, Appendix C](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-appendixes.pdf)).

Copper, from two editions of the same publication:

* 2019: world reserves 870 million tonnes, mine production 20 Mt/yr, about 43 years of supply ([MCS 2020](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2020/mcs2020-copper.pdf))
* 2025: world reserves 980 million tonnes, mine production 23 Mt/yr, about 43 years of supply ([MCS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-copper.pdf))

Roughly 130 Mt of copper was mined in between and reserves still rose. Identified resources hold about 2.1 billion tonnes (including 600 Mt already produced) and undiscovered resources an estimated 3.5 billion tonnes, as of the 2015 assessment. Lithium moved the same way faster: reserves 17 Mt (2019) to 37 Mt (2025), identified resources 80 Mt to 150 Mt, production 77,000 t to 290,000 t/yr ([MCS 2026 lithium](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-lithium.pdf)). This is why "N years of reserves left" figures barely move across decades, and why reading them as a countdown is wrong.

**Grades and energy.** A global study of copper mines found average ore grade falling about 25% over the ten years analysed, while energy use rose 46% against a 30% rise in production ([Calvo, Mudd, Valero & Valero 2016](https://doi.org/10.3390/resources5040036)). USGS attributes part of the 5% drop in US mine output in 2025 to lower ore grades. The bill lands on [energy](../energy/) and [water](../water/).

**Demand.** Under the IEA Stated Policies Scenario, demand to 2040 grows fivefold for lithium, doubles for nickel and graphite, rises 50-60% for cobalt and rare earths, and 30% for copper ([Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-critical-minerals-outlook-2025/overview-of-outlook-for-key-minerals)). Announced projects leave copper about 25% short by 2035, with lithium and cobalt also in deficit, while 2025 investment in critical minerals fell 9%, battery-metal spending over 20% and exploration over 10% ([Outlook 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-critical-minerals-outlook-2026/executive-summary)).

**Refining is more concentrated than mining.** In 2025 the top refining country averaged a 72% share across tracked minerals, up from 70% in 2023 (IEA). China refined 14.0 Mt of the world's 29.0 Mt of copper (48%) while mining 1.8 Mt of 23 Mt (8%). China mined 270,000 t of 390,000 t of rare earths (69%) and holds 44 Mt of the >75 Mt of reserves. Morocco holds 50 of 73 billion tonnes of phosphate rock reserves (68%) on 36 Mt of 250 Mt of 2025 production, which makes fertiliser a [politics](../politics/) question before it is a geology one. See also [food](../food/).

**Lead time.** Across 127 mines that started production 2002-2023, discovery to first output averaged 15.7 years; for mines starting 2020-2023 it was 17.9 years ([S&P Global, 2024](https://cdn.ihsmarkit.com/www/pdf/0724/SPGlobal_NMA_DevelopmentTimesUSinPerspective_June_2024.pdf)).

**Recycling.** Of some 60 metals surveyed, fewer than a third have an end-of-life recycling rate above 50% and 34 are below 1% ([UNEP International Resource Panel, 2011](https://www.resourcepanel.org/reports/recycling-rates-metals)). The IEA expects the recycled share of supply to rise from roughly 10% to 20% by 2040.

**Truly lost.** Helium in large-volume uses is seldom recycled ([MCS 2026 helium](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-helium.pdf)); vented, it leaves the atmosphere for good. Identified resources outside the US are about 31.3 billion m3, over half in Qatar and Algeria.

**Where the data is weak.** Reserve totals aggregate company reports written to different standards, so they are estimates of commercial intent as much as of rock. Long-run grade series are thin outside copper, Australia and Chile. And specific depletion forecasts have a poor record: in the 1980-1990 [Simon-Ehrlich wager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon%E2%80%93Ehrlich_wager) all five metals chosen by Ehrlich fell in real price. The evidence supports a problem of rate and concentration, not of the earth running out.
